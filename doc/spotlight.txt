1. INTRODUCTION                                       *spotlight-introduction*

Spotlight focuses your attention by dimming everything in the current buffer
except the regions you pick. A region can be:

    - A motion or text object (it is an operator, like |d| or |y|)
    - A Visual selection: character-wise, line-wise or block-wise
    - An Ex range

You can light one region or several at once, switch regions off and on, and
step the focus from one region to the next (|spotlight-regions|). Regions
follow your edits, are shown in every window on the buffer, and are tracked
independently per buffer.

==============================================================================
2. REQUIREMENTS                                       *spotlight-requirements*

    - Vim 8.2 or later, or Neovim 0.5 or later.
    - In Vim, the |+textprop| feature lets the region follow edits. Without
      it the plugin still works, but the region stays on fixed line and
      column numbers while you edit.

==============================================================================
3. USAGE                                                     *spotlight-usage*

With the default mappings:

    - In Normal mode, <leader>h followed by a motion or text object -
      anything you could give to |d| or |y|:
>
        <leader>h9j     current line and the 9 below
        <leader>hi{     inside braces (the braces stay dim)
        <leader>hap     a paragraph
<
    - In Visual mode, select text and press <leader>h.
    - In block-wise Visual mode (|CTRL-V|), select a column and press
      <leader>h to light just that rectangle.
    - While a spotlight is on, <leader>h adds more regions the same way
      (|spotlight-regions|).
    - <leader>hc clears everything.

The operator can be repeated with |.|.

SEVERAL REGIONS                                            *spotlight-regions*

When something is already lit, <leader>h adds another region instead of
replacing it, with the same motions, text objects and Visual selections. So
you can light several places at once:
>
    <leader>hip         light this paragraph
    <leader>h}          ...and also up to the next blank line elsewhere
    <leader>hi{         ...and also the inside of these braces
<
Everything outside all of them is dimmed. Regions that overlap or touch form
one lit area, and each one follows your edits. Deleting all of a region's
text removes that region.

<leader>hx switches the region under the cursor off: it is dimmed like the
rest of the buffer but remembered. <leader>hx on it again switches it back
on. With nested regions, the innermost one is switched. When every region is
off, nothing is dimmed.

<leader>hn lights only the next region and moves the cursor to it, pressing
it again moves on to the one after it, and stepping past the last region
lights all of them again. <leader>hb goes back the other way. Regions are
visited in buffer order and the ones switched off are skipped. Adding a
region or switching one off or on lights all of them again.

<leader>hc or |:SpotlightClear| removes every region. To start over with a
single region, use <leader>hc first, or |:Spotlight| with a range.

==============================================================================
4. COMMANDS                                               *spotlight-commands*

                                                                  *:Spotlight*
:[range]Spotlight       Spotlight the lines in [range] and dim everything
                        else, replacing any other region. [range] defaults
                        to the current line.
>
                            :5,20Spotlight
                            :.,+9Spotlight
                            :'<,'>Spotlight
<
:Spotlight!             Clear the spotlight in the current buffer.

                                                               *:SpotlightAdd*
:[range]SpotlightAdd    Add the lines in [range] as another region, keeping
                        the ones already lit. See |spotlight-regions|.

                                                             *:SpotlightClear*
:SpotlightClear         Clear the spotlight (every region) in the current
                        buffer.

                                                      *:SpotlightToggleRegion*
:SpotlightToggleRegion  Switch the region under the cursor off or on.

                                             *:SpotlightNext* *:SpotlightPrev*
:SpotlightNext          Light only the next region; past the last one, light
:SpotlightPrev          all of them again. :SpotlightPrev goes backwards.

All commands can be followed by |:bar|.

==============================================================================
5. MAPPINGS                                               *spotlight-mappings*

Everything hangs off one key, <leader>h: followed by a motion (or in Visual
mode) it lights a region, followed by c, x, n or b it acts on the regions.
See |spotlight-configuration| to change the keys and
|g:spotlight_no_default_mappings| to turn them all off.

NORMAL MODE                                              *spotlight-normal*

<leader>h{motion}       Spotlight the text covered by {motion}, or add it as
                        another region when something is already lit. Works
                        with any motion or text object:
>
                            <leader>h9j      9 lines below
                            <leader>h5k      5 lines above
                            <leader>h}       up to the next blank line
                            <leader>hi{      inside braces
                            <leader>ha(      around parens (including them)
                            <leader>hit      inside an HTML tag
                            <leader>hi"      inside quotes
                            <leader>hap      a paragraph
                            <leader>hiw      inner word
<
                        A line-wise motion (j, k, }, ap, ...) lights whole
                        lines, so text you add at the end of the last line
                        stays lit. A character-wise motion (iw, i{, ...)
                        lights exactly the covered characters.

<leader>hc              Clear every region.

<leader>hx              Switch the region under the cursor off or on.

<leader>hn              Light only the next region and jump to it; past the
                        last one, light all of them again.

<leader>hb              Same as <leader>hn, going back.

                        Because of these, <leader>h cannot be followed by the
                        motions n ("to the next match") or b ("a word back").

VISUAL MODE                                              *spotlight-visual*

<leader>h               Spotlight the selection, or add it as another region
                        when something is already lit.

                        Character-wise (|v|): dims everything before the
                        start and after the end, including the parts of the
                        first and last line outside the selection. Respects
                        'selection'.

                        Line-wise (|V|): lights whole lines.

                        Block-wise (|CTRL-V|): dims the screen columns left
                        and right of the block on every line in the block,
                        leaving a rectangle lit. Columns are screen columns,
                        so tabs and wide characters line up the way the
                        selection looked. A block extended with |$| stays lit
                        to the end of every line. Example:
>
                            <C-v>5j6l<leader>h
<
Visual mode only; Select mode is left alone so typing over a selection (for
example a snippet placeholder) never triggers it.

<PLUG> MAPPINGS                                             *spotlight-plug*

These are always defined, whatever the default-mapping settings:

    <Plug>(SpotlightAddOperator)  Normal mode: operator that lights a
                                  region, or adds one. Default <leader>h.
    <Plug>(SpotlightAddVisual)    Visual mode: same for the selection.
    <Plug>(SpotlightClear)        Normal mode: clear every region.
    <Plug>(SpotlightToggleRegion) Normal mode: switch the region under the
                                  cursor off or on.
    <Plug>(SpotlightNext)         Normal mode: focus the next region.
    <Plug>(SpotlightPrev)         Normal mode: focus the previous region.

One region at a time, as in 1.x:

    <Plug>(SpotlightOperator)     Normal mode: operator that replaces the
                                  spotlight; when one is on, clears it.
    <Plug>(SpotlightVisual)       Visual mode: replace with the selection.
    <Plug>(SpotlightToggle)       Visual mode: spotlight the selection, or
                                  clear when the buffer already has one.

To use your own keys:
>
    let g:spotlight_no_default_mappings = 1
    nmap ,s  <Plug>(SpotlightAddOperator)
    xmap ,s  <Plug>(SpotlightAddVisual)
    nmap ,sc <Plug>(SpotlightClear)
<
Use |:xmap|, not |:vmap|, for the Visual-mode target.

6. CONFIGURATION                                     *spotlight-configuration*

All options are read when they are used, except the mapping options, which
are read when the plugin loads (set them in your vimrc).

                                                  *g:spotlight_mapping*
g:spotlight_mapping     Default: '<leader>h'
                        Key used for the default Normal and Visual mode
                        mappings. The keys below default to this key
                        followed by a letter.

                                                 *g:spotlight_clear_mapping*
                                                *g:spotlight_toggle_mapping*
                                                  *g:spotlight_next_mapping*
                                                  *g:spotlight_prev_mapping*
g:spotlight_clear_mapping       Default: |g:spotlight_mapping| followed by c
g:spotlight_toggle_mapping      Default: |g:spotlight_mapping| followed by x
g:spotlight_next_mapping        Default: |g:spotlight_mapping| followed by n
g:spotlight_prev_mapping        Default: |g:spotlight_mapping| followed by b
                        Keys that clear every region, switch the region
                        under the cursor off or on, and focus the next and
                        previous region. Set one to '' to leave it unmapped.

                                             *g:spotlight_no_default_mappings*
g:spotlight_no_default_mappings
                        Default: 0
                        Set to 1 to define no default mappings. Map your own
                        keys to the |spotlight-plug| targets.

                                                  *g:spotlight_priority*
g:spotlight_priority    Default: 100
                        Priority of the dimming matches (see |matchadd()|).
                        Highlights with a higher priority show through the
                        dim; raise this if another plugin's highlights do.

                                                  *g:spotlight_hide_search*
g:spotlight_hide_search Default: 0
                        Set to 1 to hide the search highlight while the
                        current buffer has a spotlight. Searching, |star|,
                        |n| and |N| keep working; matches just aren't lit.

                        'hlsearch' is a global option and |n| turns the
                        highlight back on after |:nohlsearch|, so the plugin
                        turns 'hlsearch' off while a spotlight is shown and
                        restores it when you clear the spotlight or leave the
                        buffer. If you turn 'hlsearch' on yourself in the
                        meantime, your setting is kept.

                                        *g:spotlight_suppress_cursor_events*
g:spotlight_suppress_cursor_events
                        Default: 0
                        Set to 1 to add CursorMoved, CursorMovedI,
                        CursorHold and CursorHoldI to 'eventignore' while the
                        current buffer has a spotlight. This stops plugins
                        and scripts that highlight the word under the cursor
                        (for example coc.nvim's document highlight) from
                        drawing over the dim.

                        Highlights that follow the cursor would otherwise
                        stay frozen, so while this is in effect the
                        spotlighted windows also hide:
                          - their |:match| highlight (what most cursor-word
                            scripts use) and |matchparen|'s :3match,
                          - coc.nvim's document highlight, including a reply
                            from the language server that arrives up to 5
                            seconds after the spotlight went on,
                          - matching-pair highlights such as vim-matchup's
                            (MatchParen*, MatchWord*).
                        The |:match| is put back afterwards, and
                        |CursorMoved| is fired once so the others update.

                        This is global: while it is in effect no plugin sees
                        these events, and |matchparen| stops updating. Only
                        the events the plugin added are removed again, so
                        your own 'eventignore' settings are kept.

7. HIGHLIGHT GROUP                                       *spotlight-highlight*

                                                            *hl-SpotlightDim*
SpotlightDim            The highlight used for dimmed text.

                        Defaults, chosen from 'background':
                            dark:   ctermfg=236 guifg=#282828
                            light:  ctermfg=253 guifg=#dadada

                        It is defined with |:highlight-default|, so your
                        colorscheme or vimrc wins:
>
                            highlight SpotlightDim ctermfg=240 guifg=#3a3a3a
                            highlight link SpotlightDim Comment
<
                        Most colorschemes clear every highlight group when
                        they load, which would also remove your setting. To
                        keep it across |:colorscheme|, set it from a
                        |ColorScheme| autocommand:
>
                            augroup my_spotlight
                              autocmd!
                              autocmd ColorScheme *
                                    \ highlight SpotlightDim guifg=#3a3a3a
                            augroup END
<
                        The default is re-applied after a colorscheme
                        clears it and when 'background' changes. A color or
                        link you set yourself is never overwritten.

8. FUNCTIONS                                             *spotlight-functions*

For use in your own mappings and scripts.

spotlight#lines({first}, {last})                        *spotlight#lines()*
        Spotlight lines {first} to {last} of the current buffer, replacing
        any other region.

spotlight#add_lines({first}, {last})                *spotlight#add_lines()*
        Add lines {first} to {last} as another region.

spotlight#clear()                                       *spotlight#clear()*
        Clear the spotlight in the current buffer.

spotlight#is_active()                               *spotlight#is_active()*
        Return 1 when the current buffer has a spotlight (any region, even
        if switched off), 0 otherwise.
        Useful in a statusline:
>
            set statusline+=%{spotlight#is_active()?'[spot]':''}
<
        Note that calling it loads the plugin's autoload script.

==============================================================================
9. HOW IT WORKS                                          *spotlight-internals*

|matchadd()| attaches a highlight group to a pattern in one window. The
plugin builds patterns for everything outside the lit regions - the lines
before, between and after them and, depending on the kind of region, the
parts of a line outside it or the screen columns left and right of a block -
and gives them the SpotlightDim group. The regions themselves have no
pattern, so they keep their normal colors.

The regions are stored per buffer in b:spotlight. To follow your edits each
one is anchored with a |text-property| in Vim or an extmark in Neovim, which
move with the text. After every change the patterns are rebuilt from the
anchors in every window that shows the buffer. A region whose anchor
disappears while the others keep theirs had its text deleted and is dropped;
if every anchor disappears at once (for example when |:edit!| reloads the
file) the last known lines are kept.

The plugin does not use marks, |:match|, or any other state of yours. The
autoload script is only loaded once you use the plugin.

10. TROUBLESHOOTING                                *spotlight-troubleshooting*

<leader>h does nothing ~
    You already had a mapping for exactly <leader>h; the plugin never
    overwrites it. Pick another key with |g:spotlight_mapping|, or map a
    |spotlight-plug| target yourself. Check with:
>
        :verbose nmap <leader>h
<
<leader>h followed by n or b does something else ~
    <leader>hn and <leader>hb focus the next and previous region, so they
    cannot spotlight "to the next match" or "one word back". Use Visual mode
    for those, or move the focus keys with |g:spotlight_next_mapping| and
    |g:spotlight_prev_mapping|.

Highlights from another plugin show through the dim ~
    Raise |g:spotlight_priority|. For highlights that follow the cursor, see
    |g:spotlight_suppress_cursor_events|.

The dimmed text is too dark or too bright ~
    Set your own color, see |hl-SpotlightDim|.
